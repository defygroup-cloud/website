AI for independent agencies: why they’re consolidating and what changes

Jungle combines four firms. AI is redefining what scale means for independent agencies, and merging is no longer the only way to get it.

Which AI trends should an independent agency follow so it doesn’t fall behind? The one that matters most isn’t a particular tool but a shift in scale. AI makes execution cheaper and moves value towards research, strategy and coordination between specialisms. That’s why independents are joining forces. The good news is that scale is no longer measured in headcount alone.

October has sent a clear signal from Spain. According to El Publicista, Jungle has created Jungle Design Industries, bringing four firms under one structure: Move (branding), Redbility (digital design and user experience), Lúcid (industrial and service design) and Invisible (strategy, research and futures). Together they employ 100 people across Madrid, Barcelona and the Basque Country, and turned over €12m in 2025.

The shared logic is to pool capabilities to compete with those who have more. The interesting part is the role AI plays in that logic, and what it means for agencies that don’t want to merge, or can’t.

Why are independent agencies joining forces?

Three reasons keep coming up. The first is breadth: big clients want fewer suppliers and more integration. According to El Publicista, the firms in the new group work for brands such as BBVA, Inditex, CaixaBank, Iberdrola, Repsol, Telefónica and Moeve. With clients like that, four separate firms carry less weight than one firm with four disciplines.

The second is measurement. The article cites the Spain Design Index 2026: 52.6% of Spanish companies have no indicators to assess design’s impact on their results, in a market worth more than €1bn a year. Whoever can prove results holds a huge advantage.

The third is AI. Jungle says it plainly: artificial intelligence is transforming working processes and cutting execution times, which makes research, strategic decisions and the coordination of specialisms matter more. Jaime Bisbal, who heads the new group, puts it this way: design “has stopped being a specialism” and become “a strategic lever that influences how companies innovate”.

Is AI the cause or the excuse?

There’s a comfortable way to read all this: AI changes everything, so merging is inevitable. That’s technological determinism, the idea that technology on its own dictates how companies and society organise themselves. It’s partly right. When production gets cheaper, value moves elsewhere and structures rearrange themselves.

But technology pushes; it doesn’t decide. AI doesn’t force anyone to merge. It forces one question: where does your value sit now? If it sits in execution, you have a margin problem. If it sits in understanding people better than anyone else, AI is a multiplier.

That’s why today’s chaos has a way out. Agencies that connect their data with what they know about people find clarity: which services to keep, which to automate, who to partner with. Those that simply pile up tools pile up noise.

What “scale” means when execution gets cheaper

For decades, scale meant headcount. More people, more offices, more accounts. The holding groups added another layer: millions invested in analytics, data and AI, which they then sell to clients as an advantage. Against that, a 40-person independent didn’t stand much of a chance.

Now scale is starting to mean something else: shared intelligence. A common layer of data on audiences, competitors and creative that every discipline can tap into. There are three ways to get that layer, and none of them is free.

Which AI trends should independent agencies be watching?

Forget the lists of fifty tools. For an independent, these are the five trends that move margin and win accounts:

  • Agents for repetitive delivery. Monthly reports, competitor audits, budget tracking, resizing assets. It’s the work that eats the most hours and makes the least difference.
  • Data you query by conversation. The team asks the data questions as it would a colleague, from the assistant it already uses, instead of learning yet another dashboard.
  • Traceable conclusions. Insights that can be checked against the real posts behind them. When the client asks “where does this come from?”, the answer is one click away.
  • Creative assessed before spend. Scoring a piece’s click likelihood before paying for media changes the conversation about results.
  • Measuring impact. That 52.6% of companies with no indicators for design is an opportunity for any independent that can prove what it contributes.

How to decide your route in five steps

  1. Audit where the hours go. For a month, separate execution time from thinking time. The ratio will tell you how much margin is at stake.
  2. List what clients ask for that you don’t have. Are you short of disciplines and people, or of data and tools? They’re different problems with different solutions.
  3. Decide what to build, what to buy and what to use as a service. Developing and maintaining your own data and AI stack is exactly the cost independents can’t afford. That’s where Defy’s proposition sits: the same analytical and AI capability as the holding groups, without the cost of building it or the burden of maintaining it. You can see how it fits together in the Defy platform.
  4. Pilot it on one account for a quarter. Measure hours freed up, quality of deliverables and the client’s reaction.
  5. Only then look at mergers or alliances. With data from your own operation you’ll know what you’re genuinely missing, and you won’t buy size to paper over a tooling problem.

What doesn’t change

Consolidation will carry on. There will be more deals like Jungle’s. But the underlying reason isn’t that AI makes people unnecessary. It’s that AI exposes who brings judgement and who only brings hours.

Independents have always competed on the same things: closeness, craft and the nerve to tell the client what they don’t want to hear. AI replaces none of that. It takes the timesheets, the monthly reports and the hand-built competitor audits off people’s desks, so talent can get on with the work that wins accounts.

The insight
In connecting data to humanity, clarity emerges from chaos.

Technological determinism holds that technology dictates how companies are organised. AI is pushing consolidation, but clarity arrives when an agency connects its data with what it understands about people.

Frequently asked questions

Why are independent agencies merging in Spain?

For three main reasons: big clients want more integrated services, major accounts demand size, and AI is shortening execution times, which shifts value towards research and strategy. Jungle Design Industries, which according to El Publicista brings together four firms and 100 people, follows that logic of breadth.

Does an independent agency need to merge to compete on AI?

Not necessarily. Merging brings breadth and size, but it isn’t the only way to access analytical capability. Today an independent can use intelligence and automation platforms that already exist, without building or maintaining its own stack. The decision depends on whether what you’re missing is disciplines and people, or data and tools.

Which agency tasks should you automate with AI first?

The repetitive ones that eat lots of hours and make little difference: performance reports, competitor audits, budget tracking or adapting formats. They’re easy to measure before and after, and they free up time for strategy and creativity. The prudent approach is to start with a pilot account and measure hours saved and the quality of the output.

What is technological determinism, and why does it matter to agencies?

It’s the idea that technology on its own determines how companies and society are organised. Applied to agencies, it leads people to assume AI will make certain mergers or cuts inevitable. That reading is incomplete: technology changes where value sits, but each agency decides how to reorganise itself to capture it.
