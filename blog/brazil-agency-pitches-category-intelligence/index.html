Agency pitches in Brazil: the new rules reward those who bring evidence

Brazil's Abracom and ABA have updated their pitch recommendations. Transparent criteria favour agencies that can prove their case: here's how to build a category dossier that convinces.

The question behind this article

Key takeaways

  • The updated Abracom and ABA guide has 10 steps and calls for detailed briefs, transparent evaluation criteria and feedback for every participating agency.
  • If your proposal looks like everyone else's, the decision comes down to price, which is precisely what the guide says hiring shouldn't be.

How do you use consumer behaviour to win an agency pitch? By turning up with evidence, not opinion. The agency that shows what the category's audience is saying, what the client's competitors are publishing and where nobody is talking at all stands out from the pack. And that is exactly what Brazil's new pitch recommendations now reward.

On Tuesday 13 October, Abracom (the Brazilian association of communications agencies) and ABA (the Brazilian advertisers' association) presented the updated version of their Guide to Purchasing Corporate Communications Services, first launched in 2023. According to Meio & Mensagem, Brazil's leading marketing trade title, the document gains 10 practical steps, guidance on the use of AI and a recommended payment term of up to 60 days. The line every agency should hear comes from Carina Almeida, Abracom's director of data and market benchmarks: hiring "cannot be reduced to an automated comparison of prices and deliverables".

Translated into everyday terms: if your proposal looks like the other three, it will be compared on price. The way out is to bring something into the room that no procurement spreadsheet can compare: understanding.

What changes in agency pitches with the updated guide?

The guide was written for buying corporate communications, but the message applies to any independent agency pitching against bigger outfits. Several of the 10 steps directly change how you should prepare:

Guide stepWhat it asks forHow to respond with evidence
Brief and challengesMore detailed briefs and proposal scopesRewrite the challenge in the consumer's own words, taken from real conversations
Transparency and evaluationSelection criteria and methodology shared with the agenciesShow the whole chain: data, tension, insight, idea
Portfolio, cases and capabilitiesAssessing whether the agency builds technology into its processesShow how you track the client's category, not just your list of tools
Budget and pitch processClient discloses the investment; agency confirms the budget before taking partSize the proposal effort to the declared budget
Detailed feedback for every participantIndividual feedback on each agency's performanceUse the feedback to calibrate your next dossier

On AI, the guide acknowledges that generative AI is already used in briefs, in organising pitches, in analysing proposals and in tracking results. But it treats the technology as a tool that improves the process, not one that removes the need for human interaction. Carina Almeida sums it up: these are "predominantly intellectual and relational services".

Why has consumer behaviour become a pitch argument?

Because transparent criteria favour those who can prove their case. When the client discloses how it will judge, the presentation stops being a talent show and becomes a test of reasoning. And reasoning without data is just opinion with a nice layout.

Expectation confirmation theory helps explain the mechanism. Someone's satisfaction depends on the gap between what they expected and what they got. Every agency promises to "understand the consumer", so the client walks into the room already expecting it. The agency that shows real conversations, named tensions and competitors' ads side by side doesn't just confirm that expectation: it exceeds it. The agency that merely asserts it disappoints without even realising.

The theory has a second half that few agencies consider. The expectation set during the pitch becomes the yardstick for the first months of the account. Promising on gut feel is signing up for a disappointment next quarter.

Which consumer behaviour trends should you take into the room?

Forget the generic "trends for 2027" deck. The committee will probably see that slide in other presentations the same week. What counts is the trend in the client's own category, with chapter and verse.

Imagine a 25-person independent agency pitching for a supplements brand. Instead of "consumers are looking for wellbeing", it arrives with three findings: the question that comes up most in competitors' comments, the promise every ad repeats and the complaint nobody answers. Each finding comes with real posts attached. The conversation shifts from "we like the idea" to "how did you find that out?"

Signals worth looking for:

  • Recurring tensions: the conflict the audience expresses in its own words.
  • Shifts in language: terms, comparisons and references the audience has started to use.
  • Patterns in competitors' ads: offers, messages and formats everybody repeats.
  • White space: questions the audience asks that no brand answers.

How to build a category dossier before the pitch

  1. Confirm the budget before you start. The guide itself recommends it. A good dossier costs senior hours; don't spend them on a pitch with no declared investment.
  2. Map the whole category. Direct and indirect competitors, plus the creators who set the agenda in the conversation. The client doesn't only compete with the names in the brief.
  3. Read competitors' ads and organic content. List messages, offers and formats. Look for what everyone says in the same way.
  4. Listen to the audience's conversations. Comments, questions, complaints. Group them into personas and tensions, not age brackets.
  5. Separate pattern from anecdote. Every conclusion must point to the posts that support it. If it doesn't, it comes out of the deck.
  6. Tie each finding to the brief. Three well-connected opportunities beat twenty curiosities.
  7. Open up your methodology. The guide asks the client for transparency. Return the favour by showing where each conclusion comes from.

What does the 60-day payment term have to do with your preparation?

More than you might think. According to Meio & Mensagem, Carina Almeida argues that long payment terms shift the financing of the client's operations onto the agency, squeeze working capital and the capacity to invest, and put smaller agencies at a disadvantage in pitches.

The same logic applies to pitch effort. An independent agency doesn't have the cash to burn weeks of senior time on every pitch. When the category groundwork takes hours rather than weeks, there's energy left for what actually decides the account: the idea, the strategy and the chemistry with the client.

For Almeida, the market is moving away from cost-centred procurement towards a broader assessment of the value communication creates. That's good news for anyone who can demonstrate value before being hired. And bad news for anyone still relying solely on a pretty PowerPoint.

The insight
Understanding is the foundation of connection.

Under expectation confirmation theory, the client judges the agency by the gap between what it expected and what it received. In a pitch, showing with proof that you understand the consumer is what turns expectation into trust.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Abracom and ABA guide apply to every agency pitch?

It was written for purchasing corporate communications services and sets out best-practice recommendations. But it was updated jointly by Brazil's communications agencies' association and its advertisers' association, which gives it weight. Transparent criteria, a declared budget and feedback for every participant are principles any agency can ask for and use to its advantage.

What is category intelligence in a pitch?

It's a picture of the market the client competes in: what competitors advertise and publish, what the audience says, which tensions keep coming up and what space nobody occupies. In a pitch, it turns strategy from opinion into a verifiable argument and shows the client that the agency understands the business before it has even been hired.

Can I use AI to prepare for a pitch?

You can, and increasingly you should. The guide acknowledges that generative AI is already used in briefs, in organising pitches, in analysing proposals and in tracking results. The key is to use AI to gather and organise evidence, leaving interpretation, strategy and the client relationship, which the guide treats as essential, to people.

How do I show the client my data is reliable?

Show where it comes from. For each conclusion, present the posts, comments or ads that support it, explain how the data was collected and make clear what's a pattern and what's an exception. Since the guide asks clients to disclose their evaluation criteria, opening up your own methodology fits the spirit of the process and builds trust.
