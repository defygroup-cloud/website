Closed-loop AI for agencies: from eleven tools to one shared layer

Fragmented tools leave agency AI guessing. One shared layer of memory, skills, workflow and approvals, fed by a closed loop, gives people and AI the full picture.

Clients notice when agencies get this wrong. Campaign reports that the Ingenuity Pulse report finds only one in three marketers is satisfied with the results of their martech investments, and that this dissatisfaction is becoming a risk to agency relationships. Agencies that cannot show value from the tech stack are more exposed to losing clients.

An agency founder at an AI adoption session in Singapore this week summed up the internal version of the same problem on a single slide: eleven tools, twenty owners, nothing shared.

Why do more AI tools leave agencies knowing less?

Because every tool knows a slice and nobody owns the whole. The social listening tool knows the conversation. The ad platform knows the spend. The CRM knows the contact history, when someone remembers to log it. The chatbot knows whatever was pasted into it this morning.

In a 20-person agency, each tool tends to have a champion. The performance lead owns the bidding scripts. The social manager owns the listening dashboard. The account director's real client knowledge lives in WhatsApp. Each person's stack works for them. None of it works for the agency, and none of it works for AI.

The founder had lived it: 18 months trying to make his agency AI-native, and a sprawl of tools and skills that didn't talk to each other. His fix was not another tool. It was a layer underneath them.

What is a shared layer for agency AI?

As the diagram shows, the scattered tools on the left feed into one shared layer with four parts. The labels are his; the agency examples are ours.

LayerWhat it meansAgency example
MemoryOne source everything readsBriefs, call notes, decisions and performance data in one place, not five
SkillsThe repeatable jobs, encodedThe competitor audit, the monthly report and the creative brief, written once and reused
WorkflowHow the work actually movesBrief to concept to approval to launch, mapped from how the team really works rather than the SOP
ApprovalsA person signs before it shipsA named lead signs off budget changes, client reports and anything public

The order matters. Skills without memory produce generic output, because they don't know the client. Workflows without approvals ship mistakes faster. Memory is the foundation: everything else reads from it.

What makes an AI system closed-loop?

A closed loop means every step of a process leaves a record that the next step, human or AI, can read. An open loop has gaps: someone didn't log the call in the CRM, or the real decision happened in a private WhatsApp thread, which the founder noted is common in Asia. Each gap is missing context, and AI fills missing context with guesses.

He walked through his own new-business process as an example of a closed loop, with every stage feeding one CRM:

  • Prospect: the first email or WhatsApp message is logged, not left on someone's phone.
  • Sales call: an AI note-taker captures the conversation, and actions flow into the project-management tool.
  • Audit: an agent workflow runs the prospect audit using that context.
  • Proposal: drafted from the audit, then signed off by a human.
  • Pitch: notes are captured, including the questions the client asked, and fed back in.

His sharpest point was about questions. If you didn't ask the budget on the call, the AI doesn't know the budget. A closed loop changes how people behave in meetings, because they are now asking on behalf of the system as well as themselves.

Why does fragmentation keep happening?

Information Asymmetry Theory describes what happens when one party knows more than another: decisions get worse, trust erodes, and the better-informed side holds the power. Economists usually apply it to buyers and sellers. Agencies live with it internally every day.

The account director knows the client's real budget; the planner doesn't. The planner knows the audience insight; the creative team gets a summary of a summary. And the AI knows least of all, because it only sees what made it into a system. The client relationship has the same problem at a larger scale. When a client can't see what their martech is doing for them, as the Campaign report suggests many can't, the agency carries the doubt.

The insight behind this piece is short: collaboration conquers inefficiency. A shared layer is collaboration made structural. It narrows the gap between what one person knows and what everyone, including the AI, can use.

How do I build a closed loop in my agency?

  1. Start with one workflow. New business is a good candidate: high value, repeatable and usually leaky.
  2. List every place information enters. Email, WhatsApp, calls, meetings, shared drives, platform dashboards. Be honest about the private channels.
  3. Choose one memory. Usually your CRM or project-management tool. Agree that if it isn't there, it didn't happen.
  4. Write the questions for every call. Budget, timings, decision-makers, what success looks like. If the question isn't asked, the answer can't be logged.
  5. Capture conversations properly. Use an AI note-taker where everyone has agreed to it, and write up in-person meetings straight afterwards.
  6. Encode the repeatable jobs as skills. The audit, the proposal outline and the report, each built to read from memory.
  7. Put a named person on every approval. Proposals, budget changes and client-facing outputs get a human signature.
  8. Review the gaps monthly. Where did AI lack context? Trace it back to the step that didn't log, and fix that step.

Frequently asked questions

What is a closed-loop AI system in an agency?

It is a set-up where every step of a process, from first contact to delivery, leaves a record in a shared memory that the next step can read, whether that next step is a person or an AI agent. Nothing important lives only in a private chat or someone's head, so AI works with full context instead of guessing.

Do we have to replace our existing tools to build a shared layer?

Usually not. The shared layer is about agreeing one memory that everything reads from and writes to, encoding the repeatable jobs and adding approvals. Many agencies can build it around a CRM or project-management tool they already use. The harder change is behavioural: logging consistently and retiring tools that duplicate each other.

How should agencies handle client conversations on WhatsApp?

Treat them as part of the record. Messaging apps are a common channel for client contact, especially in Asia, but decisions made there are invisible to the rest of the team and to AI. Agree with clients which channels are used for what, and log summaries of key decisions into the shared memory, in line with your data-protection obligations.

What should we capture on every client call?

Whatever the next step will need. For new business that typically means budget, timings, decision-makers, competitors and what success looks like. Write the questions down in advance, because if the budget was never asked, neither your team nor your AI will know it when the proposal is drafted.
